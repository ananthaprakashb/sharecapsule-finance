Technical architecture

Build useful market research.
Know less about the investor.

Ticker Watch is designed around a simple boundary: the research service needs to know which public company you asked about, not the size and composition of your brokerage portfolio. The architecture combines local-first preferences, encrypted device sync, public-market evidence, source-linked interpretation, and browser-generated briefings.

System overview

Five layers, with an explicit data boundary at each step.

The architecture is intentionally easier to explain than a black-box recommendation engine. Each layer has a narrow responsibility, and the user can move from interpretation back to evidence.

Privacy architecture

Minimize first. Encrypt the state that still needs to move.

Local preference layer

Encrypted watchlist

Ticker preferences are part of the privacy-first browser experience. Optional cross-device synchronization is designed around ciphertext rather than a readable watchlist database.

  • AES-GCM encrypted sync payload.
  • Relay is not intended to read ticker symbols inside the encrypted payload.
  • Readable state exists on the trusted device when needed by the UI.

Trusted-device layer

One-time pairing

Pairing is separated from the public market research request. A short-lived pairing capability is used to connect another trusted device without turning the relay into the keeper of the vault passphrase.

  • Short-lived pairing capability.
  • Separate sync authorization.
  • Revision/conflict handling avoids silent overwrite assumptions.

Public research layer

Minimal market requests

Only the company the user asks to research needs to cross the public-market boundary for that request. This is a design choice, not merely a privacy-policy statement.

  • No brokerage login required for Ticker Watch.
  • No position size required.
  • No full portfolio needed to retrieve company research.

Presentation layer

Browser-generated briefing

The browser turns the already-displayed research into a spoken sequence, prioritizing the stories the UI has identified as significant or directional.

  • Transcript assembled from displayed items.
  • Original sources remain visible.
  • Local system voice is preferred when the browser reports one.

Cryptographic details and limitations are documented separately in the published security model.

Explainability boundary

Evidence, interpretation and prediction are different things.

Ticker Watch is deliberately structured so that a user can inspect the distinction instead of receiving one opaque “AI score.”

1

Evidence

A news story, filing or other public-market item associated with the selected company.

2

Direction

A description of whether the recent coverage appears potentially positive, negative or neutral/mixed for the company.

3

Impact

An estimate of event significance used to help prioritize reading. High impact does not mean high expected return.

4

Source

A path back to the original article or filing so the user can verify material information rather than trusting the label alone.

There is intentionally no “prediction” layer in this architecture.Direction and impact help organize research. They do not claim that a security will rise, fall, outperform or generate a particular return.

Threat model

What the design helps reduce—and what it cannot solve.

Designed to reduce

Unnecessary central knowledge.

  • Readable portfolio collection merely to research a ticker.
  • Readable watchlists sitting in the sync relay.
  • Ad-tech dependence on a detailed financial profile.
  • Unexplained interpretation detached from evidence.
  • Silent last-writer-wins assumptions during trusted-device sync.

Not solved by architecture alone

Endpoint and source risk still exist.

  • A compromised device or browser can expose information while it is readable.
  • Malicious extensions, malware, keylogging or screen capture remain endpoint risks.
  • Public data can be delayed, incomplete or wrong.
  • Interpretive labels can be wrong and must not replace source review.
  • Independent security review remains important before broad production-hardening claims.

Why this architecture matters commercially

Monetize research value, not a user's portfolio profile.

The same boundaries that support privacy can become product differentiation. A paid research product can charge for faster synthesis, deeper history, alerts, timelines and workflow—not for harvesting detailed holdings to build an advertising profile.

Architectural direction, not a claim of current availability: the normalized evidence and catalyst layer can later support paid research history, “since I last checked,” scheduled briefings, team research views, developer APIs or enterprise integrations while keeping the same evidence/provenance model. Each future capability should preserve the minimum-data boundary appropriate to its use case.
