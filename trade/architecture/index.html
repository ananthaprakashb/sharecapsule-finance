Technical architecture

Build useful market research.
Know less about the investor.

Ticker Watch is designed around a simple boundary: the research service needs to know which public company you asked about, not the size and composition of your brokerage portfolio. The current architecture combines local-first preferences, encrypted research memory, optional encrypted device sync, public-market evidence, source-linked interpretation, and browser-generated Research Radio.

Privacy architecture

Minimize first. Encrypt the state that still needs to persist or move.

Local preference layer

Encrypted watchlist

Ticker preferences are part of the privacy-first browser experience. Optional cross-device synchronization is designed around ciphertext rather than a readable watchlist database.

  • AES-GCM encrypted sync payload.
  • Relay is not intended to read ticker symbols inside the encrypted payload.
  • Readable state exists on the trusted device when needed by the UI.

Local continuity layer

Encrypted research memory

“Since I last checked” stores a per-ticker checkpoint encrypted with the existing Ticker Watch device key.

  • Prior evidence identifiers remain device-local.
  • First check creates an honest baseline rather than labeling everything new.
  • Subsequent checks compute only the newly surfaced delta.
  • Current version is intentionally not synced across devices.

Trusted-device layer

One-time pairing

Pairing is separated from the public market research request. A short-lived pairing capability is used to connect another trusted device without turning the relay into the keeper of the vault passphrase.

  • Short-lived pairing capability.
  • Separate sync authorization.
  • Revision/conflict handling avoids silent overwrite assumptions.

Public research layer

Minimal market requests

Only the company the user asks to research needs to cross the public-market boundary for that request. This is a design choice, not merely a privacy-policy statement.

  • No brokerage login required for Ticker Watch.
  • No position size required.
  • No full portfolio needed to retrieve company research.

Audio presentation layer

Research Radio in the browser

The browser turns already-displayed evidence into a spoken sequence. It prioritizes high-impact and directional items and can include SEC filings in longer formats.

  • 3-, 5- and 10-minute target formats for single-ticker research.
  • Actual runtime depends on available sourced evidence and playback speed.
  • Changes-only Plus mode consumes the local research-memory result.
  • Local system voice is preferred when the browser reports one.

Entitlement layer

Free research, paid continuity

The commercial boundary follows the architecture: free users retain useful single-ticker research and audio; Plus adds research memory and automation.

  • Billing checks access independently of market evidence retrieval.
  • Free research is not hidden behind an entitlement request.
  • Plus monetizes continuity and saved time rather than access to basic evidence.

Cryptographic details and limitations are documented separately in the published security model.

Research Radio architecture

Listening is another view of the evidence—not another black box.

Research Radio does not invent a separate recommendation layer. It transforms the same visible research into a prioritized spoken sequence and keeps the source links in the transcript.

Explainability boundary

Evidence, interpretation and prediction are different things.

Ticker Watch is deliberately structured so that a user can inspect the distinction instead of receiving one opaque “AI score.”

1

Evidence

A news story, filing or other public-market item associated with the selected company.

2

Direction

A description of whether the recent coverage appears potentially positive, negative or neutral/mixed for the company.

3

Impact

An estimate of event significance used to help prioritize reading. High impact does not mean high expected return.

4

Source

A path back to the original article or filing so the user can verify material information rather than trusting the label alone.

There is intentionally no “prediction” layer in this architecture.Direction and impact help organize research. They do not claim that a security will rise, fall, outperform or generate a particular return.

Threat model

What the design helps reduce—and what it cannot solve.

Designed to reduce

Unnecessary central knowledge.

  • Readable portfolio collection merely to research a ticker.
  • Readable watchlists sitting in the sync relay.
  • Readable research-history checkpoints stored by the market service.
  • Ad-tech dependence on a detailed financial profile.
  • Unexplained interpretation detached from evidence.
  • Silent last-writer-wins assumptions during trusted-device sync.

Not solved by architecture alone

Endpoint and source risk still exist.

  • A compromised device or browser can expose information while it is readable.
  • Malicious extensions, malware, keylogging or screen capture remain endpoint risks.
  • Public data can be delayed, incomplete or wrong.
  • Interpretive labels can be wrong and must not replace source review.
  • Research memory is device-local today, so another device may have a different checkpoint.
  • Independent security review remains important before broad production-hardening claims.

Current product boundary

Free evidence. Paid continuity and automation.

The architecture supports a business model in which users can evaluate the research quality before paying. Plus monetizes repeated effort that the system can remove.

Why this architecture matters commercially

Monetize research value, not a user's portfolio profile.

The same boundaries that support privacy can become product differentiation. A paid research product can charge for continuity, synthesis, alerts, timelines and workflow—not for harvesting detailed holdings to build an advertising profile.

Future architectural direction: the normalized evidence and provenance layer can later support encrypted cross-device research-memory sync, team research views, developer APIs or enterprise integrations. Those capabilities are not represented here as currently available, and each should preserve the minimum-data boundary appropriate to its use case.
