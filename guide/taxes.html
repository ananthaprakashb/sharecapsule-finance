Tax control

Taxes are a year-round cash-flow item, not an April surprise.

Good tax control means estimating liability early enough to change withholding, make estimated payments and avoid creating a cash-flow shock at filing time.

Tax-control routine

1. Estimate annual incomeInclude wages plus interest, dividends, capital gains, self-employment or other taxable income that may not have withholding.
2. Compare expected withholdingCheck year-to-date withholding and projected remaining pay periods.
3. Recheck after changesMarriage, divorce, child, home purchase, second job, retirement or income changes can alter tax needs.
4. Separate tax cashFor income without sufficient withholding, avoid treating the future tax amount as spendable cash.
5. Coordinate investment decisionsRealized gains, losses, dividends and retirement distributions can change tax outcomes.
6. Review before year-endDo not wait until the return is being prepared to discover a withholding gap.
ShareCapsule opinion

A large refund is not automatically a financial success, and a balance due is not automatically a failure. The better objective is predictable cash flow with enough paid during the year to avoid an unpleasant bill or penalty while not withholding far more than necessary.

When to check withholding

IRS guidance recommends checking withholding early in the year, when tax law changes, and after major life or income changes. The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator uses current pay information to estimate federal income-tax withholding and can help determine whether a new Form W-4 or W-4P may be appropriate.

New job, second job or spouse starts/stops working.
Marriage, divorce, birth/adoption or retirement.
Meaningful interest, dividends, capital gains or self-employment income.
Large change in deductions or credits.
Unexpected prior-year refund or tax bill.

Tax-advantaged contributionsRetirement, HSA or other eligible contributions and current annual limits.
Investment taxesRealized gains/losses, dividends, distributions and holding periods.
RecordsReceipts and documentation needed for deductions, credits, basis or business expenses.
