Credit control

Use credit cards as a payment tool, not an income source.

Credit cards can improve convenience, protections and rewards, but carrying expensive revolving balances can overpower those benefits quickly.

The control hierarchy

  1. Never miss the required payment. Use reminders or automatic minimum payments as a safety net.
  2. Prefer paying the statement balance in full by the due date. When a card has a grace period, this generally avoids purchase interest.
  3. If you carry a balance, stop optimizing rewards. APR and payoff speed matter much more than points or cash back.
  4. Track every card in one table. Record issuer nickname, credit limit, statement balance, APR, due date, annual fee and promotional-expiration date.
  5. Direct extra debt payments by strategy. The avalanche approach sends extra dollars to the highest APR after required minimums are covered elsewhere.
ShareCapsule opinion

If a household is carrying revolving credit-card debt at a high APR, earning 2% rewards should not influence spending decisions. Eliminating a 20%+ APR balance is usually a much more powerful financial improvement than chasing card rewards.

Utilization signals

Healthy habitPay in full when possible

Keep balances comfortably below limits and avoid using available credit as part of the monthly budget.

WatchApproaching 30%

CFPB notes that experts commonly advise keeping use below about 30% of total available credit; lower may be preferable for score optimization.

ActionRepeatedly near the limit

This can signal a cash-flow problem and can also hurt credit scores. Focus on spending and payoff before opening more credit.

Card-by-card review

QuestionWhy it mattersOur action
Do I pay the full statement balance?Carrying balances may trigger purchase interest and eliminates much of the value of rewards.If no, rank debt by APR and reduce discretionary card spending.
Is a 0% offer expiring?Promotional and deferred-interest offers have different terms and can become expensive.Record the exact expiration date and payoff requirement.
Does the card have an annual fee?A fee is worthwhile only if benefits you actually use exceed the fee and do not cause extra spending.Review once per year before renewal.
Am I applying for cards frequently?CFPB advises applying only for credit you need; repeated new accounts can affect scores.Pause applications unless there is a clear purpose.
Have I reviewed my credit reports?Errors or fraud can affect borrowing costs.Review reports regularly and dispute inaccuracies.

When cash flow is tight

If you cannot make the minimum payment, act early. CFPB recommends contacting the card issuer immediately, explaining why you cannot pay, what you can afford, and when normal payments may resume. Be cautious with companies promising to make debt disappear or telling you to stop communicating with lenders.

List all minimum payments and due dates.
Protect housing, food, utilities, transportation and insurance first.
Call issuers before missing payments.
Avoid new discretionary charges while a payoff plan is active.
Track progress monthly, not just the total debt balance.
