Long-term planning

Retirement planning is a savings-rate problem before it becomes an investment-selection problem.

Use employer benefits and tax-advantaged accounts deliberately, then invest those accounts according to a diversified long-term plan.

Contribution ladder

  1. Capture available employer match. Understand vesting rules and the contribution needed to receive the full match.
  2. Maintain essential liquidity and control high-cost debt. Do not create a fragile household just to maximize a retirement contribution.
  3. Use tax-advantaged space appropriate to eligibility. Workplace plans and IRAs have different limits, tax treatment and withdrawal rules.
  4. Increase contributions with raises. A small automatic increase each year can materially change long-term results.
  5. Coordinate account allocation. Treat the household portfolio as one system rather than managing each account in isolation.
ShareCapsule opinion

Automating contribution increases is one of the most useful retirement habits because it converts future income growth into long-term savings before lifestyle spending absorbs it.

Retirement review questions

Am I receiving the full employer match available to me?
What percentage of gross income is going toward retirement across employee and employer contributions?
Is the portfolio diversified across asset categories and within them?
Are fees reasonable for the strategy being used?
Are old employer accounts being managed intentionally rather than forgotten?
Have beneficiaries been reviewed after marriage, divorce, birth or other major life changes?
